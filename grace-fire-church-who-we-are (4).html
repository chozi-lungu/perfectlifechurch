Statement of Faith

Our foundational doctrinal convictions and scriptural pillars

1. The Scriptures Inspired

The Bible is the inspired, only infallible, and authoritative Word of God — our sufficient rule for faith and practice (2 Timothy 3:15–17; 2 Peter 1:21).

2. The One True God

There is one God, eternally existent in three persons: God the Father, God the Son, and God the Holy Spirit (Deuteronomy 6:4; Matthew 28:19).

3. The Deity & Humanity of Jesus Christ

Jesus Christ is fully God and fully man, born of a virgin, lived a sinless life, performed miracles, died on the cross for our sins, bodily resurrected, and ascended to the right hand of the Father (Hebrews 1:3; 1 Corinthians 15:3–4).

4. The Fall & Salvation of Man

Salvation is a gift received by grace through personal faith in Jesus Christ's shed blood. Regeneration by the Holy Spirit is absolutely essential for personal salvation (Ephesians 2:8–9; Titus 3:5).

5. Water Baptism & The Lord's Supper

We observe water baptism by immersion as an outward sign of inward faith, and the Holy Communion in remembrance of Christ's sacrifice (Matthew 28:19; 1 Corinthians 11:23–26).

6. The Baptism in the Holy Spirit

All believers are promised and should ardently expect the baptism in the Holy Spirit with the initial physical evidence of speaking in tongues as the Spirit enables (Acts 1:8; Acts 2:4).

7. Sanctification & Holiness

We are called to live holy, set-apart lives through the daily empowering presence of the Holy Spirit (Romans 12:1–2; 1 Thessalonians 5:23).

8. Divine Healing

Deliverance from sickness and affliction is provided in the atonement and is the privilege of all believers through expectant prayer (Isaiah 53:4–5; James 5:14–16).

9. The Blessed Hope

The imminent, personal return of Jesus Christ to resurrect the saints and consummate His eternal Kingdom (1 Thessalonians 4:16–17; Revelation 21:1–4).

About Tithing & Giving

Biblical foundation, practical breakdown, and a tithe calculator

Tithing is not a church rule — it's a biblical principle that predates the law of Moses. Here are the three scriptures that define how we understand giving at Perfect Life Christian Church.

Malachi 3:10
"Bring the whole tithe into the storehouse, that there may be food in my house. Test me in this," says the Lord Almighty, "and see if I will not throw open the floodgates of heaven and pour out so much blessing that there will not be room enough to store it."
The foundational tithe verse. Notice God says "test me" — tithing is one of the few places in Scripture where God invites us to prove Him. The "storehouse" in context is your local church — the place where you are spiritually fed.
2 Corinthians 9:7
"Each of you should give what you have decided in your heart to give, not reluctantly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver."
This is why we never pressure anyone to give. God is not interested in your money if it comes with resentment. A gift given joyfully — whether it's 10% or 1% — carries more weight than a grudging tithe. Start where your faith is.
Luke 21:1–4
"As Jesus looked up, he saw the rich putting their gifts into the temple treasury. He also saw a poor widow put in two small copper coins. 'Truly I tell you,' he said, 'this poor widow has put in more than all the others.'"
Jesus never measured giving by the amount — He measured it by the sacrifice. God sees your heart before He sees your bank account. If you're in a hard season, even a small faithful gift is honoured.
Our position: We teach tithing as a biblical principle and a spiritual discipline — not as a condition of membership or a measure of your faith. You are welcome here whether you tithe or not. We simply want you to understand what Scripture says and give you the freedom to respond.

Many people use these words interchangeably — but they mean different things. Understanding the difference helps you give with intention.

The Tithe
10%
The first 10% of your income returned to God through your local church. It's not a donation — it's a return. "The tithe belongs to the Lord" (Lev 27:30). Goes into the General Fund to keep the church running.
The Offering
Above 10%
Whatever you give above the tithe — freely and cheerfully. Offerings can be designated to specific causes: missions, building fund, youth ministry. This is where generosity moves from discipline into worship.
Firstfruits
First & best
Your first income from a new job, business, harvest, or year — given to God before anything else. It's a declaration that He is your source. Proverbs 3:9 — "Honour the Lord with your wealth, with the firstfruits of all your crops."

Gross or net? We don't prescribe — that's between you and God. Some people tithe on gross (before tax) as a declaration of faith. Others tithe on net (after tax) as their practical starting point. Both are honourable. Start where you are.

Honest answers to the questions people are actually thinking but sometimes afraid to ask.

Do I tithe on my gross income or net (after tax)?
Scripture doesn't specify gross or net because tax systems didn't exist in the same way. What matters is the principle of the first and the best. Many mature givers tithe on gross as an act of faith — but if tithing on net is where your faith is today, start there. God is not a tax auditor. He's a Father who sees your heart.
I'm unemployed or going through a hard season. Should I still tithe?
Give what you can, even if it's not 10%. The widow Jesus commended gave two copper coins — not a tithe. God sees your faithfulness, not your balance sheet. If you have nothing coming in, pray and seek the church's support — that's also what the storehouse is for. You don't have to figure this out alone.
Can I split my tithe between this church and another ministry?
The tithe in its biblical context was brought to the "storehouse" — the local place of worship where you are spiritually fed. We believe your tithe belongs at the church you call home. Giving to other ministries is wonderful — but that's your offering, above the tithe, not instead of it.
What does the church actually do with tithes and offerings?
We publish an annual financial report available to all members. In short: pastoral salaries, church operations and utilities, children's and youth ministry, community outreach, missions partnerships, and the building fund. 100% stays in ministry — no external shareholders, no personal gain.
Is tithing a New Testament practice or just Old Testament law?
Abraham tithed to Melchizedek in Genesis 14 — before the law. Jesus affirmed tithing in Matthew 23:23 without abolishing it. The New Testament raises the standard of giving — not lowers it. The principle of returning the first tenth to God predates Moses and points forward to Christ. We see it as a timeless kingdom principle, not a legal obligation.
Am I less of a Christian if I don't tithe?
No. Your salvation is by grace through faith — full stop. Tithing is a spiritual discipline, not a condition of grace. We teach it because we believe it unlocks something in your relationship with God and your trust in Him as provider. But we will never make you feel like a second-class member for where you are on this journey.
